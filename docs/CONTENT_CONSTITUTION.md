# Zenz Content Constitution

Approved by the founder on 2026-09-05. Supersedes every prior content plan,
calendar, script, hook, caption and creative direction, including the 25-day
mystery campaign drafted 2026-08-25. Those are discarded, not paused.

This document governs Instagram (@zen_z.app) and any content that follows.
Product rules still live in PRODUCT_SPEC.md and ARCHITECTURE.md. Visual and
copy bans still apply.

---

## 1. Who we are creating for

**The person the content is about:** a JNU student who is socially fine and
quietly out of sync. They have a group, they sit with them every day, and
their references land in an empty room. Not lonely, not friendless. Never
flatten this into "make new friends."

**The person who spreads it:** the always-online, trend-aware students. They
may not have the feeling. They have the reach.

**The person we must win first:** women at JNU. A male-skewed first month ends
the product on one campus. From the first post, a female student must be able
to conclude that the person running this is safe, serious, and not building a
way to meet girls. This reorders who we speak to, not just what we say.

**Not for:** anyone looking for a date.

## 2. What Zenz stands for

You can be surrounded by people and still not have found your people. And at
JNU specifically, nothing much happens, so nobody ever gets the chance to.

The promise stays small and honest: not "we will find your people," but "we
will put you at a table where the odds are good." One evening, four strangers,
chosen by a person who read your answers.

## 3. What we are trying to achieve

Campus conversation at JNU for 30 days, in this order: they talk about the
concept, they recognise the feeling, they recognise our formats, they know the
founder.

Downloads are the byproduct. Accepted cost: launch-day numbers will look modest
next to reach. We do not panic in week two.

Fixed date: the college fest, 29 September. Launch lands around 20 to 25
September.

## 4. Content personality

A specific, observant student telling you something true, quickly, without
performing.

Honest before clever. Funny when it is actually funny. Confident, never
motivational. Comfortable with a plain sentence and a quiet moment. Self-aware
enough to mock our own app, our own follower count, our own awkwardness.

**Humour rule: borrowed format, real observation.** Trending formats,
templates and audio bits are fair game. What goes inside them has to be true
about student life. A trending template with a real observation is not cosplay.
A trending template with a generic "college students be like" line is.

## 5. Tone of voice

- First person, spoken the way the founder speaks.
- **Language:** speak however the sentence wants, Hindi, English, or the mix
  you would use with a friend. On-screen text stays English by default so it
  travels past JNU, except when a line is a Hindi line and translating would
  kill it.
- The hook is the truest line in the piece, moved to the front. Never
  manufactured tension, never fake mystery, never a countdown for its own sake.
- Feelings described plainly, never dramatised.
- Never: "revolutionising," "join our community," "Gen Z," rocket emojis,
  "book now," "limited slots," "verified students," em dashes, the "it is not
  X, it is Y" construction.
- We never claim we will find you your people. We claim we will build you a
  good table.

## 6. Visual direction

Shot on phones, real campus, real light, no studio. Editing is clean and
restrained, colour graded, honest cuts. Motion graphics are saved for a few
signature moments rather than sprinkled everywhere. Text overlays are
typographic and legible on mute. The red Z and the brand faces appear
sparingly, at end frames and as format markers, never stamped on everything.

Music per piece. Trending audio when it genuinely helps reach, voice and
ambient sound when the piece is better naked. No forced trending sound.

Consistency comes from voice and format, not a locked grid. We experiment for
three weeks and let the look emerge.

## 7. Content pillars

**1. Out of Sync, 30%.** The feeling, first person. The founder can say his
friends do not understand his references. We never portray them as lesser, and
we never cast actors as "the friends who do not get it."

**2. The Table, 25%.** The mechanic and the human behind it. The quiz, why you
cannot choose who you sit with, the reveal, the venue, and the fact that the
founder builds every group by hand and can only do 10 to 20 a week. Carries
trust and safety, and answers the woman-at-JNU question by showing the founder
rather than by writing a policy caption. The refund promise lives here.

**3. Nothing Happens Here, 30%.** A campus where every week is the same week.
One third JNU-specific, concentrated on that theme, two thirds general student
and hostel life. Where the humour lives and where reach comes from.

**4. Come Sit, 15%.** The invitation, the capture mechanism, the fest, launch,
and eventually real tables and real people. Rises to dominate launch week,
drops back after.

Most posts open in 1 or 3 and land in 2 or 4. These are not four separate
content types taking turns.

## 8. Formats we prioritise

1. **Founder talking-head, phone, one take.** Cheapest and most on-brand.
2. **Campus approach content.** Real students on camera. Load-bearing, since
   there is no recurring team cast. Highest reach, manufactures material on a
   campus where little happens, doubles as recruitment.
3. **Text over b-roll.** Cheap, carries pillar 1, ships without the founder.
4. **Stories, daily.** Not counted against the quota. Home of the capture
   mechanism and unpolished access.
5. **Carousels, sparingly.** Only for ideas that need reading.
6. **One-off team appearances.** No recurring characters. Teammates appear
   when a piece needs them.

## 9. Things we will never do

- Never make Zenz look like a dating app, never position against one. Dating
  apps appear only as an occasional punchline that kills the confusion.
- Never suggest students see each other's photos.
- Never invent scarcity. Ours is real: one person builds every table.
- Never name a venue before assignment. No location picker, ever.
- Never touch religion, caste, national politics, named individuals, anyone's
  appearance, or a specific student's social life. No contextual exception.
- Never portray the founder's friends, the team, or users as lesser.
- Never fake vulnerability. Bugs and bad days do not automatically become
  content.
- Never post filler to hit a number.
- Never show a table, a user, or a testimonial we did not actually have.

## 10. The founder's role

Narrator, not subject. His face and name carry the content. The content is
about the thing, not about him. Success is a stranger saying "you made that
video about the cafe," not "you are the Zenz guy."

His story is fully available: Surat, the move, family, the stand-up show. The
stand-up show is the strongest single asset and is deliberately held back
rather than spent in week one. The crying stays out unless he decides
otherwise. The team hears the real version from him, in person, before we
shoot anything built on it.

With no recurring cast, he is the only consistent face, so we batch shoot in
the daily two-hour team block and stockpile.

## 11. Growth philosophy

- **10 posts a week.** Roughly 5 to 6 Reels, the rest lighter, Stories daily on
  top. Mandatory review at day 14: if quality is dropping or the editor is the
  bottleneck, we cut to 6 and that is not failure.
- **Judged on profile visits, saves, shares.** Not likes, not comments. The
  honest content will have quiet comment sections because nobody publicly
  admits this feeling. Expected, not failure.
- **Three posts, then kill it.** No ceremony.
- **Instagram will not deliver a campus.** Posters, the fest, QR T-shirts and
  the confessions page matter as much as the feed.
- **Capture beats follows.** A follow decays in three weeks. A phone number
  does not.

## 12. The biggest opportunities

1. **"Nothing happens at JNU."** Nobody says it, everybody feels it, and it
   walks directly into the product.
2. **The price.** Rs 126 for a movie with four strangers, Rs 70 for eight-ball,
   nothing more to pay. Cheaper than a boring evening.
3. **The refund promise.** Full refund plus the next one free removes the
   scariest part of booking.
4. **29 September.** The largest concentration of the audience all semester,
   days after launch, with T-shirts, QR codes and freedom to roam.
5. **The confessions page.** Our emotional territory already has an audience
   on campus.
6. **Honest scarcity.** "About 15 tables a week, because I build them myself."
7. **The +1.** The answer to the real objection, which is not price or safety
   but not wanting to walk in alone.

## 13. The biggest risks

1. **Male skew.** The one that ends it.
2. **Ten a week eats the honesty.** Cadence and taste collide around week three.
3. **The web build.** iPhone students meeting a broken browser experience means
   content manufactured the damage.
4. **Attention with nowhere to go.** Every day without a capture mechanism is
   wasted reach.
5. **Campus approach content not happening.** Load-bearing and the thing the
   team fears most.
6. **Hook-chasing drifting into what the founder hates.**
7. **Over-promising belonging.** The product delivers one evening.
8. **Founder bandwidth.** With no recurring cast, almost nothing ships on a day
   he cannot shoot.

## Product facts content may state

- Convenience fee Rs 21 for cafes and dinners. The student's own cafe/dinner
  spend is separate and budget-matched at booking.
- Movie: Rs 126, nothing further to pay.
- Cricket: Rs 221 for 2 hours. Football: Rs 221 for 1 hour. Eight-ball: Rs 70
  for 1 hour. Pickleball: Rs 129 for 1 hour. Prepaid.
- No-show: full refund, and the next booking is free.
- Group preference: men only, women only, or mixed. Plus a +1 for anyone who
  does not want to arrive alone.
- In-app reporting exists for any group member.
- Founder builds every group by hand. Ceiling is roughly 10 to 20 groups/week.
- iPhone users use the web version at launch. Android via the store.
