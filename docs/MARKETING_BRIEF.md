# The Project — A Brief for Marketing

*Written for a teammate joining on the marketing side — no code, no jargon, just what this thing is and why it exists.*

---

## The one-liner

A campus-first app that turns "let's meet new people" into an actual event. Students book a time slot for a Café, Dinner, or Movie. We (the founder) hand-craft a group of 4-5 compatible strangers for that slot. They get revealed to each other, chat a bit, and show up.

No swiping. No browsing profiles. No choosing who to meet — that's the whole point.

## The problem we're solving

Campus social life defaults to the same 5 people. Everyone wants to meet new people but nobody wants the awkwardness of a dating-app-style browse-and-match, and nobody has time to organize a group hangout from scratch. We remove the browsing *and* the organizing — you just show up to something someone else planned for you.

## Who it's for

College/university students, first launch focused on campus communities. The person booking isn't looking for a date — they're looking for a low-effort, slightly mysterious social evening.

## How it actually works (the student's path)

1. **Sign up** with just an email (no student ID, no verification, no phone number required for login) — deliberately zero-friction.
2. **Build a profile**: name, year, gender, a photo. The photo is explained on the spot: *"seen only by our team, never by other members."* That line matters — see Trust below.
3. **Take a short personality quiz** — a handful of questions about how they behave socially (not a Buzzfeed quiz, it actually feeds the matching).
4. **Pick a slot**: Café, Dinner, or Movie, a day/time, a budget band, and whether they want a mixed group or a women-only group. That's it — no picking *where*, no picking *who*.
5. **Pay a small unlocking fee** to confirm the booking.
6. **Wait** — the app deliberately doesn't show "3/5 spots filled," it shows an ambiguous "something is coming" state. Anticipation is the product here, not transparency.
7. **Get matched** — founder manually builds the group of 4-5 using the quiz data + a drag-and-drop admin tool. Compatibility, not just availability, drives who ends up together.
8. **Reveal** — students see their groupmates' first name, year, and a short blurb. No photos. The venue itself stays hidden until ~2 hours before the event.
9. **Group chat** opens so the group can coordinate before meeting IRL.
10. **They meet up**, then leave feedback afterward.

## The brand voice

This is the single most important thing for marketing to internalize: **the tone is mysterious and adventurous, never operational or transactional.** We're not a booking app and copy should never read like one.

| Instead of saying... | We say... |
|---|---|
| "Booking confirmed" | "Your invitation is sealed." |
| "Your group is ready, tap to meet them" | "The story begins here. Tap to meet your group." |
| "See you in 2 hours!" | "Two hours until your next adventure." |
| "Rate your group" | "How did your story end tonight?" |

Onboarding lines set the tone: *"Every table has a story before anyone sits down."* / *"We craft your group. You just show up."* / *"No swiping. No searching. Just an invitation."*

Anything that sounds like a delivery-app notification or an event-ticketing platform is off-brand. Marketing copy, ads, and social content should borrow this same voice — think "invitation" and "story" language, not "book now" and "limited slots."

## What makes it different (the pitch angles)

- **Curated, not swiped.** A real person (the founder) builds every group by hand using personality data — this isn't an algorithm matching you to a stranger's dating profile photo.
- **Photo privacy is a real trust feature, not a compliance checkbox.** No student ever sees another student's photo — not in chat, not at reveal, nowhere. Only the founder's team sees photos, purely to help build better groups. This is a genuinely strong "we're not another dating app" talking point — students meet people for who they are, not their photo grid.
- **All three formats live from day one** — Café, Dinner, Movie are never "coming soon." Marketing can promote any/all of them without caveats.
- **The mystery is deliberate.** Not knowing the exact group or venue until close to the event is a feature we're leaning into, not a limitation to apologize for.
- **Founder-run, not automated.** Early messaging can lean into the "hand-picked" / concierge feel — a real person is behind every group, which is a good story for a small, trust-building launch.

## Trust & safety, in plain terms (good to know, careful how it's marketed)

- Sign-up is just an email — no student ID or photo ID check. This lowers friction but means we don't verify someone is who they claim to be beyond an email address. Founder-only photo visibility and manual moderation are the real safety net, not ID verification. Worth knowing before marketing makes any "verified students only" style claims — we should not imply an identity-verification guarantee we don't have.

## Business model basics

- Students pay a small fee to unlock/confirm each booking (via PayU).
- There's a referral program: invite a friend, earn a ₹21 credit once they take their first step in (their first booking gets paid). That fully covers a Café or Dinner; on a pricier activity (Movies, Sports) it's ₹21 off, not the whole thing — capped deliberately so a cheaply-earned credit can't fully waive an expensive booking. Unlimited invites, one credit per friend who books.

## Where the build actually stands today

Built and working: sign-up/login, profile + photo upload, the personality quiz, all three activities visible on the home screen, the 5-step booking flow, payments, and the founder's admin dashboard (including the drag-and-drop matching board).

Not yet built: the automated side of matching (still manual via the dashboard), in-app group chat, push notifications, reporting/moderation tools, and analytics. So — good time to talk about the *concept* and the *vibe*, a bit early to promise specific in-app features like chat or push reminders as live today.

## A few guardrails for anything public-facing

- Never suggest there's a location/venue picker for students — the founder assigns venue, always, and only reveals it shortly before the event.
- Never say or imply photos are shared between students under any circumstance.
- Don't promise ID/student-verification as a safety feature — it doesn't exist in this version.
- Keep copy in the "invitation / story / adventure" register, not "book / confirm / slot" register.
